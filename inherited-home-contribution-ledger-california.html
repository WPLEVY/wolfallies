Each sibling completes their own copy before family conversations about the inherited home.

Educational worksheet only. This ledger helps organize information for family discussion. Whether documented contributions create legal entitlements depends on your specific trust, will, agreements, and California law. This is not legal advice. Consult a qualified California attorney before making claims based on this documentation.

This ledger provides a structured way to document contributions each family member made to a parent's care, the family home, or the estate before or after death. Each sibling completes their own copy privately, then the family reviews together at the meeting or mediation.

The purpose is not to create legal claims. It is to give everyone the same visibility into what actually happened, so the family conversation can be based on shared information rather than competing recollections.

Using This Ledger

Once completed, this ledger becomes an input to family discussion, not the final answer. The values shown may or may not translate into unequal distribution depending on the trust document, family agreement, and legal analysis.

Recommended next steps after completing the ledger:

  • Share with the family before the meeting. If each sibling completes their ledger separately and shares in advance, everyone comes to the meeting with the same information.
  • Discuss without accusation. The ledger is a summary of contributions, not a claim of unfair treatment. Treat it as information.
  • Review with an attorney. An attorney can advise which contributions might have legal weight (documented debts, caregiving contracts) versus which are purely moral (undocumented care).
  • Consider mediation for disagreement. If family members reach different conclusions from the same information, a professional mediator can help find middle ground.
A Note on Fairness

Documenting contributions carefully sometimes changes minds. Siblings who thought their contributions were unrecognized often feel heard when they see the numbers. Siblings who thought they contributed more may see they contributed similarly. The point is not to prove someone wrong. The point is to have the conversation grounded in facts rather than feelings.
