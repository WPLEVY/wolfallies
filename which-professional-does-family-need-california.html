Inherited California Real Estate: Which Professional Does the Family Need?

By William B. Plevy, California Real Estate Broker · DRE #01956776 Download PDF →

Families dealing with inherited property usually need more than one kind of professional, and it's not always obvious who to call first. This is a quick reference for sorting that out, whether you're the family facing the decision or a professional trying to point a client in the right direction.

SituationProfessionalWhat they do
A will or trust needs to be created or updatedEstate-planning attorneyDrafts or revises the documents that determine how the property passes.
The estate must go through probate courtProbate attorneyFiles the petition, obtains Letters Testamentary, manages court confirmation of any sale.
A trust needs formal administration after deathTrust administration attorneyGuides the successor trustee through notice requirements, accounting, and distribution.
Need clarity on basis, capital gains, or filing requirementsCPADetermines stepped-up basis and files the estate's or trust's tax returns.
Need a valuation for court, taxes, or a buyoutAppraiserProvides an independent fair market value, required for probate referee reports and fair sibling buyouts.
No family member is available or willing to serve as trustee or executorProfessional fiduciaryServes as a neutral, professional trustee or executor in place of a family member.
The family disagrees about what to do with the propertyMediatorA neutral third party who helps resolve disagreements before they turn into litigation.
One heir wants to buy out the others and needs financingLenderProvides trust or estate loans structured specifically for sibling buyouts.
The property needs to be listed, marketed, or soldInherited-property RealtorHandles the sale itself, with specific experience in trust and probate requirements.

Many situations touch two or three of these at once, a probate sale usually needs both a probate attorney and an inherited-property Realtor, for example. If it's not clear which combination applies to a specific situation, that's a reasonable first question to ask before anything else moves forward.
