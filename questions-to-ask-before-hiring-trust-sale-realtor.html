Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Trust Sale Realtor in California

By William B. Plevy, California Real Estate Broker · DRE #01956776 Updated June 2026
The Short Answer

Before hiring a real estate agent for a California trust property sale, ask specifically about their trust sale transaction volume, their understanding of Certification of Trust requirements, their experience working with probate attorneys, and how they have handled beneficiary conflicts. General real estate experience is insufficient — you need demonstrated trust sale expertise.

Question 1: How many trust administration sales have you completed in California in the past 12 months? Look for a specific number, not vague assurances. An agent who has closed 10+ trust sales in the past year has meaningful experience. An agent who cannot give you a specific number probably has little.

Question 2: Walk me through the Certification of Trust — what does it need to say, when do you need it, and what happens if the title company has questions about it? An experienced trust sale agent can answer this question fluently. An inexperienced agent will look uncertain or give a generic answer about 'title company requirements.'

Question 3: Have you worked with estate attorneys on trust sales? Can you provide a reference from a probate or estate attorney who has worked with you? The best probate and trust agents have ongoing relationships with estate attorneys in their market. Attorney referrals are the strongest signal of genuine expertise.

Question 4: What is your approach to communicating with beneficiaries? Have you handled trust sales where beneficiaries had different opinions about the sale? This question reveals whether the agent understands the human dynamics of trust administration — the reality that multiple beneficiaries often have different views, and that the agent will need to communicate with all of them professionally.

Question 5: What price would you recommend for this property, and why? A trust sale specialist will be able to explain their pricing recommendation with reference to comparable sales, current market conditions, the property's condition, and how the price relates to any appraisals. Be wary of an agent who simply tells you what you want to hear.
