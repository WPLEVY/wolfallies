How Do I Clean Out an Inherited House in California Before Selling?

By William B. Plevy, CA Real Estate Broker & Attorney · DRE #01956776 Updated June 2026
The Short Answer

Cleaning out an inherited California home before sale involves systematically removing personal property over days or weeks, retaining anything of potential value for beneficiary distribution or estate sale, donating usable items, and disposing of the rest. Professional estate sale companies can handle most of this efficiently if the volume warrants it.

Start with a walkthrough to assess the volume and nature of the contents. Distinguish between: items with potential financial value (jewelry, art, collectibles, quality furniture), items with sentimental value that beneficiaries may want, items suitable for donation, and items requiring disposal. Take photos throughout — this documentation can be useful if beneficiaries later question what happened to specific items.

For properties with significant personal property, an estate sale company can manage the entire process. They assess, price, advertise, and conduct the sale — typically taking 25-40% of gross proceeds. This is often the most efficient solution for homes with decades of accumulated possessions. Get multiple quotes and check references before hiring.

For smaller volumes, donate usable items to local charities. Many will pick up furniture and household goods. Keep donation receipts — charitable contributions are potentially deductible against the estate's income.

California law has specific rules about what happens to personal property in a trust or probate estate. If specific items are mentioned in the trust or will, they must be distributed as specified. If not mentioned, personal property is typically distributed proportionally or sold with proceeds added to the estate. Consult your estate attorney before discarding anything that could have value.

Timing matters for the real estate sale. A cluttered, fully furnished home is harder to show and photograph effectively. Most real estate agents recommend staging or at minimum clearing and cleaning the home before professional photography. The cost of this preparation — estate sale, donation pickup, cleaning — is an estate expense paid from trust or estate assets.
