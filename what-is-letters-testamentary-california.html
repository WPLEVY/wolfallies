What are Letters Testamentary and Why Do You Need Them in California?

The Answer

Letters Testamentary is the court-issued document that gives a California executor legal authority to act on behalf of the estate. Without Letters Testamentary, an executor has no legal capacity to sell estate property, access estate bank accounts, or take other substantive action. The document is issued by the probate court at the initial hearing after the executor is formally appointed, typically 4-8 weeks after the probate petition is filed. Multiple certified copies are needed, banks, title companies, and government agencies each require an original certified copy.

Going Deeper

Letters Testamentary in California specify the scope of the executor's authority, including whether IAEA authority was granted. For real property sales, the title company handling the transaction will require a certified copy of Letters Testamentary to verify the executor's authority to sell. The document remains valid for the duration of the probate administration but can be revoked by the court if the executor fails to fulfill their duties.
